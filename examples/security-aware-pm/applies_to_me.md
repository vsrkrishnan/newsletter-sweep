I'm a product manager with a background in network and cloud security,
now building AI-native products. My lens is trust and abuse, not just
capability — when I read about a new agent pattern or AI feature, I'm as
interested in how it fails or gets misused as in what it does when it
works.

I'm trying to get sharper on:
- Agentic product architecture — multi-agent systems, tool use, context
  management, evals for AI features (not "AI is impressive," but how
  teams actually measure whether an AI feature is doing its job)
- AI security and adversarial AI: prompt injection, agentic attack
  surfaces, abuse of coding agents and CI pipelines — implications for
  product builders and security-aware PMs, not just security engineers
- PM craft for products in genuine decline or without market tailwinds —
  most PM content assumes growth; I'm specifically interested in the
  skills that don't get taught because of that assumption

When triaging content, favor items that are specific and actionable over
ones that are purely motivational, restate common knowledge, or make a
sweeping claim without a concrete mechanism behind it. A well-argued
counter-intuitive point is worth more to me than a confident mainstream
one.
